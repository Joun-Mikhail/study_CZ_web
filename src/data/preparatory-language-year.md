# Preparatory & Language Year in Czechia

---

## Overview | نظرة عامة

One of the smartest paths to free university education in the Czech Republic is completing a **preparatory (foundation) year** before starting your degree. By Czech law, citizens of **all nationalities** can study for free at public and state universities — but only in programs taught in Czech. A preparatory year bridges the gap: you learn the Czech language intensively and prepare for entrance exams, all within one academic year (typically September to June).

Foundation programs combine **intensive Czech language instruction** (from A0 to B2/C1) with **subject-specific preparation** (math, physics, biology, economics, etc.) tailored to your intended field of study. After completing the program, you'll be ready to apply to Czech public universities and study tuition-free.

---

## Why Do a Preparatory Year? | لماذا تعمل سنة تحضيرية؟

1. **Free university education afterward** — Once you reach the required Czech level (usually B2), you can enroll in any public university program taught in Czech at zero tuition cost.

2. **Structured language learning** — You'll study 20–35 hours per week with experienced teachers, progressing from complete beginner to B2 level in one year.

3. **Entrance exam preparation** — Programs include specialized subjects (math, physics, biology, chemistry, etc.) so you're ready for university entrance exams.

4. **Recognized certificates** — Many university programs accept the certificate from accredited preparatory courses in lieu of a separate Czech language exam. For example, the ÚJOP Charles University certificate is recognized by more than 80 faculties.

5. **Visa support** — Accredited preparatory programs at public universities qualify for a long-term study visa (purpose: "study"), making the visa process smoother.

6. **Adaptation time** — You get a full year to adjust to Czech culture, make friends, navigate bureaucracy, and find your footing before university starts.

7. **Dormitory access** — Most programs offer university dormitory accommodation (typically 3,000–7,500 CZK/month).

## Types of Programs | أنواع البرامج

### 1. Foundation Programs (Year-Long Preparatory Courses)
- **Duration:** 1 academic year (September – June), typically 2 semesters + inter-semester course
- **Intensity:** 25–35 hours/week
- **Content:** Intensive Czech language + specialized subjects for your chosen field
- **Target:** Students who want to enter a Czech-taught degree program at a public university
- **Visa purpose:** "Study" (účel pobytu: studium) — at public universities

### 2. Czech Language Courses (Semester or Year-Long)
- **Duration:** 1 semester to 1 year
- **Intensity:** 16–25 hours/week
- **Content:** Czech language only (no specialized subjects)
- **Target:** Students who want to learn Czech for general purposes or prepare independently
- **Visa purpose:** Varies — "Study" at public universities, "Other" at private institutions

### 3. Short-Term / Summer Courses
- **Duration:** 2 weeks to 3 months
- **Content:** Intensive Czech language, culture, excursions
- **Target:** Students who already have some Czech knowledge or want a short immersion
- **Visa:** Short-term Schengen visa (up to 90 days)

## Visa & Residence | التأشيرة والإقامة

### For Non-EU Students

If you're from outside the EU/EEA, you'll need a **long-term visa** or **long-term residence permit** for the purpose of study.

**Key distinction:**
- **Foundation programs at public universities** → You apply for a long-term visa/residence with purpose **"Study"** (studium). This is the best option because it gives you free access to the Czech labor market.
- **Language courses at private institutions** → You apply with purpose **"Other"** (ostatní) with specification "preparation for study." This is more restrictive.

**Documents typically required:**
- Valid passport
- Acceptance letter from the educational institution
- Proof of accommodation (usually provided by the university)
- Proof of financial means — bank statement showing at least **CZK 115,810** (~€5,000) in your account
- Health insurance for the entire stay (approximately €611/year when ordered through ÚJOP)
- Criminal record certificate from your home country
- Application form + consular fee of **CZK 2,500**

**Processing time:** Up to 60 days from submission.

**Important:** Schedule your embassy appointment early — some embassies have long waiting times. Apply at the Czech embassy/consulate in your home country or country of residence.

### For EU/EEA Citizens
You don't need a visa. Simply register at the Foreign Police within 30 days of arrival.

- **Most universities require B1–B2** level for admission to Czech-taught programs
- **Medical faculties** typically require B2 or higher
- **Recommended:** Aim for B2 to be comfortable with lectures and exams
- The preparatory year certificate from an accredited program is often accepted in place of a separate Czech language exam

## Step-by-Step: How to Enroll | خطوات التسجيل

1. **Choose your target university and field of study** — This determines which preparatory program and specialized subjects you need.

2. **Select a preparatory program** — Pick based on your target field, city preference, and budget.

3. **Apply to the preparatory program** — Submit your application with required documents (passport copy, high school diploma translation, etc.).

4. **Pay the tuition fee** — Full payment is usually required before the acceptance letter is issued. Most programs refund if your visa is refused.

5. **Receive your acceptance letter and accommodation confirmation** — The institution sends these for your visa application.

6. **Apply for your visa** — Schedule an appointment at your nearest Czech embassy/consulate. Submit all required documents.

7. **Arrange health insurance** — Purchase comprehensive health insurance for the entire period.

8. **Arrive in Czech Republic** — Register at the Foreign Police within 3 working days of arrival.

9. **Start your preparatory course** — Attend classes regularly (mandatory for maintaining your visa).

10. **Apply to university** — During the spring semester, apply to your chosen university program. Take entrance exams in May–June.

## Tips & Advice | نصائح مهمة

- **Start early:** Apply 6–8 months before the course starts (typically January–March for a September start) to allow time for visa processing.

- **Don't wait for the minimum:** Universities require B1, but from practical experience, B2 is strongly recommended. Lectures and specialized terminology are challenging even at B2.

- **Budget realistically:** Beyond tuition, plan for accommodation (3,000–7,500 CZK/month), food (~5,000–7,000 CZK/month), transport (~500–800 CZK/month), and insurance (~€611/year).

- **The bank statement requirement:** You need to show CZK 115,810 (~€5,000) in your account for visa purposes. This money is not blocked — you can use it once you arrive.

- **Nostrification is separate:** You need to get your high school diploma recognized (nostrified) in the Czech Republic. This takes 4–6 months. Start this process as early as possible — it can only be done in the Czech Republic.

- **Choose public over private if possible:** Preparatory programs at public universities give you a "study" purpose visa, which includes free labor market access and is easier to extend.

- **Attend classes:** Regular attendance is mandatory. Missing classes can jeopardize your visa status.

## FAQ | أسئلة شائعة

**Q: Can I work during the preparatory year?**
A: If you have a long-term visa/residence for the purpose of "study" (at a public university), you have free access to the Czech labor market — no additional work permit needed. If your visa purpose is "other," work restrictions apply.

**Q: Is the preparatory year considered university study?**
A: Foundation programs at public universities are legally classified as preparation for accredited university study under Czech law (Act No. 326/1999 Coll., §64b). This is why they qualify for a "study" visa.

**Q: Can I apply to any university after the preparatory year?**
A: Yes. You're not limited to the university where you did the preparatory course. You can apply to any Czech university.

**Q: What if I already speak some Czech?**
A: Most programs accept students at all levels (A0–C2). If you already have basic Czech, you'll be placed in an appropriate group and may progress faster.

**Q: What happens if I fail the entrance exam?**
A: You can usually retake exams or apply to different universities/faculties. Some programs offer support with backup applications.

**Q: Can I extend my visa after the preparatory year?**
A: Yes. Once accepted to a university, you apply for a long-term residence permit extension for the purpose of study. You'll need to show proof of enrollment and sufficient funds again.
